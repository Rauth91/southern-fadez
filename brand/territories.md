# Three design territories

A territory is a visual world, not a color theme. Pick one before implementation.

Shared constraints for all three:

- Mobile-first: most people will open this from Instagram, Maps, or a text
- One primary action: Book / Call
- Real Prairieville facts, not invented luxury
- No black-and-gold gentleman template
- No TN Southern Fades clone

---

## Territory 1 — The Line-Up (recommended)

**Thesis:** The site is built like a fade. A hard vertical edge (the lineup) holds type and navigation. Content then blends out — tight, then open — the way a fade leaves the hairline.

**Why it fits:** The name is Southern *Fadez*. The Z already signals contemporary fade culture, not a 1920s shop. Airline Hwy competitors own sports, merch, and old-school. Nobody owns the craft geometry of the cut itself.

**Visual tension:** Clipper precision × Louisiana heat. Industrial tool language against humid, informal Southern speech.

**Typography:** Condensed industrial display (clipper-housing / hardware-sign energy) + a warm, readable body face. The Z in Fadez is a mark, used sparingly.

**Composition:** Asymmetric. A persistent vertical rule on the left (desktop) that everything aligns to. Services read like a guard chart (0, 0.5, 1, 2…) rather than icon cards.

**Color / material:** Clipper housing black, blade steel, shop-towel white, one oxide red (the switch on a detailer — not gold). Fluorescent-cool light, not candlelight.

**Imagery:** Tight crops of hairlines, blends, and hands. No wide empty shop hero.

**Navigation:** The lineup *is* the nav. Short labels. Book stays attached to the edge.

**Motion:** Almost none except the blend: a controlled opacity/scale as sections enter, like a fade opening. Respect reduced motion.

**Mobile:** The vertical rule becomes a hard top edge. Type stacks. Book is always reachable.

**Signature idea:** A “guard map” of services — the menu is numbered like clipper guards, not like a spa.

**Cliché risk:** Streetwear lookbook, Andis ad, numbered 01/02/03 decoration. Numbers must be real guards / real durations, not chrome.

**Competitor distance:** Far from Mr. Carter’s, Draft Picks, Barber Bayou, and the TN site.

**Gates:** Beauty yes if the edge is disciplined. Identity yes — the lineup + guard map would look wrong on a restaurant. Familiarity: industrial type is CURRENT, not yet AI-default for barbers if gold is refused. Effect removal: the rule and type still hold without photos.

## Territory 2 — Shopfront After Dark

**Thesis:** The site is the storefront on Airline Hwy at dusk. Channel-letter honesty. Hours as a lit sign. You look through a window, not a lifestyle campaign.

**Why it fits:** Prairieville clients need to know where it is, when it’s open, and that it’s a real shop in a real strip. Trust comes from being locatable and open, not from luxury claims.

**Visual tension:** Highway commercial × sign-painter craft.

**Typography:** Storefront condensed gothic for the name. Matter-of-fact sans for everything else. Prices look like a laminated chair-side list.

**Composition:** Window frames. A large “OPEN / CLOSED” or hours block as a physical sign. Interior photography sits behind glass, slightly reflected.

**Color / material:** Asphalt, fluorescent tube, enamel red, humid night. No gold, no magnolia.

**Imagery:** The actual suite, parking, chair, highway light. If we don’t have those photos yet, type and signage carry the page.

**Navigation:** Like a painted window directory: Cuts · Fades · Kids · Book.

**Motion:** A slow dusk shift is too decorative. Instead, the open/closed state is the only “live” element.

**Mobile:** The window becomes a stacked sign — name, hours, book, then the list.

**Signature idea:** Live shop-status as the hero. “Open till 7 · Suite B · Airline Hwy” is the headline.

**Cliché risk:** Retro diner neon, Route 66, generic Americana.

**Competitor distance:** More local and physical than Draft Picks’ brochure. Less theatrical than Mr. Carter’s.

**Gates:** Beauty depends on restraint of the signage. Identity is strong if the window metaphor is specific. Familiarity: neon is SATURATED — this territory only works if the signs feel enamel/fluorescent, not glow. Effect removal: hours-as-headline still works.

## Territory 3 — Next Chair

**Thesis:** The website is the appointment. No slogan. The hero is the next available seat, duration, and price. Editorial booking ledger, not a marketing page.

**Why it fits:** Suburban clients are busy. They will bounce from a vibe site. A ledger says: we respect your time, the fade is consistent, sit down.

**Visual tension:** Ledger clarity × close-range craft photography.

**Typography:** Tabular figures as the loudest voice. A workhorse grotesque. Display type only for the name.

**Composition:** Two columns like an appointment book. Left: times, lengths, prices. Right: one photograph of the work. No card grid.

**Color / material:** Paper white, carbon ink, a single leather-chair oxblood used only for the book action. Cool, not spa-cream.

**Imagery:** One excellent crop at a time. Empty chairs if that’s the truth of the room.

**Navigation:** Minimal. Book, Menu, Hours, Place.

**Motion:** None that delays the next slot.

**Mobile:** The next slot and Book occupy the first screen. Photos wait below.

**Signature idea:** “Next chair” as a real status, not a metaphor — even if initially static (“Walk-ins welcome · typical wait 20 min” or a booking embed).

**Cliché risk:** Looks like Square Appointments or a SaaS dashboard. Cream/serif “premium reservation house” is banned here.

**Competitor distance:** More useful than every local competitor site. Less personality unless photography is strong.

**Gates:** Beauty if the ledger is typeset with care. Identity is weaker without photos — many clinics could wear a ledger. Content fit is excellent for conversion.

---

## Recommendation

**Territory 1 — The Line-Up.**

It is the only direction that could only belong to a fade shop named Fadez. It gives the site a craft identity the Airline Hwy corridor does not have, while still supporting a ruthless mobile booking path.

Borrow Shopfront’s insistence on real address/hours, and Next Chair’s refusal to hide the action under poetry. Do not borrow Shopfront neon or Next Chair’s SaaS calm as the whole look.
